# Nigerian Dietary Guidelines

## Daily Calorie Requirements (Nigerian Context)

The average Nigerian adult requires between 2,000 and 2,500 kilocalories per day, depending on age, gender, activity level, and health status. The Federal Ministry of Health recommends the following distribution:

- **Carbohydrates**: 55-65% of total calories. Nigerian diets are naturally carbohydrate-rich due to staples like rice, yam, cassava, and swallows.
- **Protein**: 10-15% of total calories. Many Nigerians do not meet the recommended daily protein intake of 0.8g per kilogram of body weight.
- **Fat**: 20-30% of total calories. Palm oil and groundnut oil are the most common cooking fats in Nigerian cuisine.

## Recommended Daily Intake for Nigerian Adults

### Males (19-50 years)
- Calories: 2,200-2,800 kcal (depending on activity)
- Protein: 56g minimum
- Iron: 8mg
- Calcium: 1,000mg
- Vitamin A: 900mcg RAE
- Vitamin C: 90mg

### Females (19-50 years)
- Calories: 1,800-2,200 kcal (depending on activity)
- Protein: 46g minimum
- Iron: 18mg (higher due to menstruation)
- Calcium: 1,000mg
- Vitamin A: 700mcg RAE
- Vitamin C: 75mg

## Nigerian-Specific Nutritional Challenges

### Iron Deficiency
Iron deficiency anemia affects over 50% of Nigerian women of reproductive age. Leafy vegetables like Ugwu (fluted pumpkin leaves), Efo Tete (African spinach), and Efo Shoko are excellent sources of non-heme iron. Consuming these with Vitamin C-rich foods like tomatoes and peppers enhances iron absorption.

### Vitamin A Deficiency
Vitamin A deficiency is prevalent in Nigeria, especially among children. Red palm oil, a staple in Nigerian cooking, is one of the richest natural sources of beta-carotene (provitamin A). Orange-fleshed sweet potatoes and dark green leafy vegetables are also excellent sources.

### Protein Gap
Many low-income Nigerians rely heavily on carbohydrate-rich staples without adequate protein. Affordable protein sources include beans (ewa), eggs, crayfish, stockfish, and snails. A single egg provides about 6g of high-quality protein at a very affordable price.

### Sodium Overconsing
Many Nigerian dishes are prepared with high amounts of salt, seasoning cubes (Maggi, Knorr), and fermented condiments (dawadawa, iru/ogiri). Excessive sodium intake is linked to the rising rates of hypertension among Nigerians. The WHO recommends less than 2,000mg of sodium per day.

## Calorie Computation: Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

For calculating Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR), the Mifflin-St Jeor equation is considered the most accurate:

- Male BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) + 5
- Female BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) - (5 × age in years) - 161

Activity multipliers:
- Sedentary (desk job, student): BMR × 1.2
- Lightly active (walking, light exercise 1-3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
- Moderately active (exercise 3-5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
- Very active (intense exercise 6-7 days/week): BMR × 1.725

## Weight Management Guidelines

### Weight Loss
A safe calorie deficit is 500 kcal per day below TDEE, leading to approximately 0.5kg weight loss per week. Never go below 1,200 kcal/day for women or 1,500 kcal/day for men without medical supervision.

Nigerian-friendly weight loss strategies:
- Choose lighter swallows like Wheat Meal or Amala over Eba
- Use Okra soup or Ewedu (low calorie) instead of Egusi or Banga soup
- Replace Fried Yam with Boiled Yam or Roasted Plantain
- Increase vegetable portions in soups
- Choose grilled or peppered protein over fried

### Weight Gain
A safe calorie surplus is 300-500 kcal per day above TDEE. Focus on nutrient-dense foods rather than empty calories.

Nigerian-friendly weight gain strategies:
- Choose energy-dense swallows like Eba or Pounded Yam with Egusi soup
- Add extra protein (meat, fish, eggs) to each meal
- Include healthy snacks like Guguru and Epa (popcorn and groundnuts)
- Drink Kunu or Tiger Nut Drink instead of water with meals

## Meal Timing for Nigerian Lifestyle

### Typical Nigerian Meal Pattern
- **Breakfast (7-9 AM)**: Usually lighter — pap and akara, bread and egg, oats, custard
- **Lunch (1-3 PM)**: The heaviest meal — rice and stew, swallow and soup, beans
- **Dinner (6-8 PM)**: Moderate — often leftovers, lighter soups, or a smaller portion of lunch-type foods

### Budget Allocation
For a typical Nigerian student or worker:
- Breakfast: 20-30% of daily food budget
- Lunch: 35-45% of daily food budget
- Dinner: 25-35% of daily food budget
