# Health Conditions and Nigerian Dietary Management

## Diabetes (Type 2)

### Overview
Nigeria has one of the highest diabetes burdens in Africa, with over 11 million adults affected. Type 2 diabetes requires careful management of blood sugar through diet, primarily by controlling carbohydrate intake and choosing foods with a low glycemic index.

### Dietary Guidelines for Diabetics
- **Carbohydrate Control**: Limit total carbohydrate intake to 45-60% of daily calories. Choose complex carbohydrates over simple ones.
- **Glycemic Index Awareness**: Prefer foods with low GI (below 55) to moderate GI (56-69). Avoid high GI foods (70+).
- **Fiber Intake**: Aim for 25-30g of fiber daily. Fiber slows glucose absorption.
- **Meal Timing**: Eat at regular intervals. Do not skip meals. Small, frequent meals help stabilize blood sugar.
- **Sugar Avoidance**: Minimize added sugars, sugary drinks (Coke, Fanta, sweetened Zobo), and sugar-heavy snacks.

### Nigerian Foods GOOD for Diabetics
- **Swallows**: Amala (yam flour, lower GI than Eba), Wheat Meal (high fiber), Oatmeal
- **Soups**: Okra Soup (low calorie, high fiber), Ewedu (very low calorie), Bitter Leaf Soup (has blood-sugar-lowering properties), Efo Riro
- **Proteins**: Grilled fish, boiled eggs, snails, lean meats (all have zero/minimal carbs)
- **Vegetables**: All leafy vegetables (Ugwu, Efo Tete, Efo Shoko, Garden Egg)
- **Beans**: All bean dishes (high fiber, moderate GI, good protein) — Beans Porridge, Moi-Moi, Ekuru
- **Fruits**: Garden Egg (very low sugar), unripe plantain

### Nigerian Foods to AVOID or LIMIT for Diabetics
- **High GI Swallows**: Eba/Garri (very high GI), Fufu, Semo
- **Sugary Foods**: Puff Puff, Chin Chin, Custard, Coke/Fanta, sweetened Zobo
- **White Rice**: Very high GI — choose Ofada Rice or Native Rice instead
- **Processed Foods**: Indomie noodles (high sodium, high GI)
- **Fried Foods**: Fried Yam, Fried Plantain (frying increases GI)
- **Sugary Drinks**: Limit fruit juices, sweetened beverages

### Recommended Daily Limits for Diabetics
- Sodium: below 2,300mg/day
- Added Sugar: below 25g/day
- Fiber: minimum 25g/day
- Carbohydrates: 130-200g/day (depending on severity)

## Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)

### Overview
Hypertension is the leading cause of cardiovascular disease in Nigeria, affecting roughly 30-40% of adults. It is often called the "silent killer" because many Nigerians are undiagnosed. Diet plays a critical role in managing blood pressure.

### Dietary Guidelines for Hypertension (DASH-style for Nigeria)
- **Sodium Restriction**: The most critical dietary change. Limit sodium to below 1,500-2,000mg/day.
- **Potassium Increase**: Potassium helps counter sodium's effects. Eat potassium-rich foods like plantain, beans, and leafy vegetables.
- **Reduce Saturated Fat**: Choose grilled/boiled protein over fried. Reduce palm oil usage.
- **Increase Vegetables and Fruits**: Aim for 5+ servings of vegetables daily.
- **Limit Alcohol**: Moderate or eliminate alcohol consumption.

### Nigerian Foods GOOD for Hypertension
- **Vegetables**: All leafy vegetables (Ugwu, Efo Tete, Efo Shoko) — rich in potassium and magnesium
- **Proteins**: Grilled fish (especially Titus/mackerel — omega-3 fatty acids lower blood pressure), snails, boiled eggs
- **Beans**: All bean dishes — high in potassium, magnesium, and fiber
- **Oatmeal**: Lowers cholesterol and blood pressure
- **Plantain**: Rich in potassium (especially boiled or roasted, not fried)
- **Swallows**: Wheat Meal (high fiber), Amala (lower sodium if prepared without much salt)
- **Soups**: Efo Riro, Okra Soup (minimize seasoning cubes)

### Nigerian Foods to AVOID or LIMIT for Hypertension
- **High Sodium Foods**: Suya (very high sodium from seasoning), processed snacks (Gala, Meat Pie), Indomie noodles (extremely high sodium — 1,200mg+ per serving)
- **Seasoning Cubes**: Maggi, Knorr, Royco — major sodium contributors. Use natural spices instead.
- **Fried Foods**: All deep-fried items (Akara, Dundun, Dodo) — increase saturated fat
- **Processed Meats**: Sausages, bacon, canned meats
- **Sugary Drinks**: Contribute to weight gain, which worsens hypertension
- **Ponmo**: While low in calories, often prepared with excessive salt

### Recommended Daily Limits for Hypertension
- Sodium: below 1,500mg/day (strict) or 2,000mg/day (moderate)
- Saturated Fat: below 13g/day
- Potassium: at least 3,500mg/day

## Pregnancy

### Overview
Proper nutrition during pregnancy is crucial for both mother and baby. Nigerian pregnant women need increased calories, protein, iron, folic acid, calcium, and vitamins. The Federal Ministry of Health recommends an additional 300-500 kcal/day during the second and third trimesters.

### Dietary Guidelines for Pregnancy
- **Calorie Increase**: +300 kcal/day in second trimester, +450 kcal/day in third trimester
- **Protein Increase**: 71g/day (up from 46g for non-pregnant women)
- **Iron**: 27mg/day (critical to prevent anemia, very common in Nigerian pregnant women)
- **Folic Acid**: 600mcg/day (prevents neural tube defects)
- **Calcium**: 1,000mg/day (for baby's bone development)
- **Vitamin A**: Important but do NOT exceed 3,000mcg/day (excess is toxic to the fetus)

### Nigerian Foods EXCELLENT for Pregnancy
- **Leafy Vegetables**: Ugwu (fluted pumpkin leaves), Efo Tete — extremely rich in iron, folic acid, and Vitamin A. CRITICAL for preventing anemia.
- **Ewedu**: Rich in folic acid, iron, and calcium. Very safe for pregnancy.
- **Eggs**: Complete protein source, rich in choline (important for baby's brain development). Safe to eat daily.
- **Beans/Moi-Moi**: Excellent source of plant protein, iron, and folic acid
- **Crayfish**: Rich in calcium, good for baby's bone development
- **Fish**: Tilapia, catfish — good protein and omega-3
- **Efo Riro/Edikang Ikong**: Nutrient-dense vegetable soups packed with vitamins
- **Oatmeal**: Good source of iron and fiber, helps with constipation (common in pregnancy)
- **Fruits**: Oranges (Vitamin C + folic acid), bananas (potassium), watermelon

### Nigerian Foods to AVOID During Pregnancy
- **Raw/Undercooked Meats**: Ensure all meats are thoroughly cooked
- **Excessive Palm Oil**: While nutritious, too much can cause excessive weight gain
- **Alcohol**: Absolutely no alcohol during pregnancy
- **Excessive Caffeine**: Limit to 200mg/day
- **Peppersoup with certain herbs**: Some traditional herbs used in peppersoup may stimulate contractions
- **Unpasteurized Dairy**: Rare in Nigeria but still a concern

### Recommended Daily Targets During Pregnancy
- Calories: TDEE + 300-450 kcal
- Protein: 71g minimum
- Iron: 27mg (nearly double the non-pregnant requirement)
- Calcium: 1,000mg
- Folic Acid: 600mcg

## Peptic Ulcer

### Overview
Peptic ulcers are common in Nigeria, often linked to H. pylori infection, stress, and dietary habits. While diet alone does not cure ulcers, it can significantly affect symptom management and healing.

### Dietary Guidelines for Ulcer Patients
- **Eat Regularly**: Never skip meals. An empty stomach increases acid production and worsens symptoms.
- **Small, Frequent Meals**: Eat 5-6 small meals instead of 3 large ones to keep stomach acid neutralized.
- **Avoid Irritants**: Spicy foods, acidic foods, and alcohol directly irritate the stomach lining.
- **High Fiber**: Fiber-rich foods protect the stomach lining and reduce acid concentration.
- **Probiotics**: Fermented foods may help fight H. pylori and support gut health.

### Nigerian Foods GOOD for Ulcer Patients
- **Oatmeal**: Soothes the stomach, high in fiber, non-irritating
- **Pap (Ogi)**: Gentle on the stomach, easy to digest, traditionally given to sick people
- **Boiled Plantain**: Non-acidic, gentle, provides energy
- **Ekuru (White Moi-Moi)**: Steamed, bland, easy to digest, no oil or pepper
- **Ewedu (Plain)**: Mucilaginous texture soothes the stomach lining
- **Okra Soup**: Slimy texture protects stomach lining
- **Boiled Eggs**: Non-irritating protein source
- **Omelette (Vegetable)**: Gentle, nutritious
- **Boiled Yam**: Bland, filling, non-irritating
- **Water**: Stay hydrated, helps dilute stomach acid
- **Garden Egg**: Alkaline, helps neutralize stomach acid
- **Bread (without butter/margarine)**: Absorbs excess acid

### Nigerian Foods to AVOID with Ulcers
- **Spicy Foods**: Suya, Peppersoup, Ata Rodo (scotch bonnet pepper), any heavily peppered food
- **Fried Foods**: Akara, Dundun, Dodo — oil irritates the stomach lining
- **Acidic Foods**: Tomato-heavy stews (can trigger symptoms in some patients)
- **Carbonated Drinks**: Coke, Fanta — gas and acidity worsen symptoms
- **Alcohol**: Directly damages the stomach lining
- **Very Hot Foods**: Temperature extremes irritate the ulcer
- **Coffee/Strong Tea**: Caffeine stimulates acid production
- **Chin Chin/Puff Puff**: Fried, sugary — double irritant

### Recommended Dietary Pattern for Ulcer Patients
- Eat every 3-4 hours
- Include a small protein source at every meal
- Drink water between meals, not during
- Avoid eating within 2 hours of bedtime
- Choose boiled, steamed, or baked foods over fried
