# Micronutrient Guide: Nigerian Foods

## Iron

### Why Iron Matters
Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency in Nigeria, especially among women and children. Iron is essential for:
- Hemoglobin production (carries oxygen in blood)
- Energy metabolism
- Immune function
- Cognitive development

### Daily Iron Requirements
- Men: 8mg/day
- Women (19-50): 18mg/day
- Pregnant women: 27mg/day
- Children: 7-15mg/day (age dependent)

### Best Nigerian Iron Sources

**Plant-Based (Non-Heme Iron)**:
| Food | Iron per Serving | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Ugwu (Fluted Pumpkin Leaves) | 5.0mg per 100g | The BEST plant-based iron source in Nigerian cuisine |
| Efo Tete (African Spinach) | 4.5mg per 100g | Widely available and affordable |
| Efo Shoko (Lagos Spinach) | 4.0mg per 100g | Common in Yoruba markets |
| Edikang Ikong Soup | 6.0mg per bowl | Premium but extremely iron-rich |
| Efo Riro | 5.5mg per bowl | Excellent iron delivery through vegetable soup |
| Beans (all varieties) | 3.5-4.5mg per serving | Also provides protein |
| Ewedu | 2.2mg per bowl | Light but iron-rich |

**Animal-Based (Heme Iron — better absorbed)**:
| Food | Iron per Serving | Notes |
|------|-----------------|-------|
| Snail (Igbin) | 3.5mg per 100g | Surprisingly iron-rich |
| Goat Meat | 3.0mg per piece | Lean and iron-rich |
| Beef/Assorted Meat | 2.5mg per piece | Common protein source |
| Liver | 6.0mg per 100g | Richest heme iron source (but limit during pregnancy) |

### Iron Absorption Tips
- Eat Vitamin C-rich foods (tomatoes, peppers, oranges) WITH iron-rich foods to boost absorption by up to 6x
- Avoid drinking tea or coffee with iron-rich meals (tannins block absorption)
- Cooking in iron pots can increase the iron content of foods
- Fermented foods like Ogi (Pap) have better iron bioavailability than unfermented versions

## Calcium

### Why Calcium Matters
Calcium is critical for bone health, muscle function, and nerve signaling. Nigerian diets are often low in calcium because dairy consumption is limited in many parts of the country.

### Daily Calcium Requirements
- Adults: 1,000mg/day
- Pregnant women: 1,000mg/day
- Adolescents: 1,300mg/day

### Best Nigerian Calcium Sources
| Food | Calcium per Serving | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| Ugwu Leaves | 150mg per 100g | Best vegetable calcium source |
| Efo Tete | 140mg per 100g | Excellent and affordable |
| Efo Shoko | 130mg per 100g | Good alternative |
| Ewedu | 120mg per bowl | Significant calcium content |
| Okra Soup | 90mg per bowl | Also rich in fiber |
| Crayfish (Ground) | 80mg per 2 tbsp | Used in almost every Nigerian soup |
| Snail (Igbin) | 80mg per 100g | Good non-dairy calcium |
| Beans | 70-80mg per serving | Also provides iron and protein |
| Eggs | 28mg per egg | Modest but consistent source |

### Calcium Absorption Tips
- Vitamin D enhances calcium absorption — get sunlight exposure (Nigeria has abundant sunshine)
- Excessive salt intake causes calcium loss through urine
- Oxalates in some vegetables can reduce calcium absorption — cooking reduces oxalate content

## Vitamin A

### Why Vitamin A Matters
Vitamin A is essential for vision, immune function, and skin health. Vitamin A deficiency (VAD) remains a significant public health issue in Nigeria, particularly among children under 5.

### Daily Vitamin A Requirements
- Men: 900mcg RAE/day
- Women: 700mcg RAE/day
- Pregnant women: 770mcg RAE/day (but DO NOT exceed 3,000mcg — excess is toxic)

### Best Nigerian Vitamin A Sources
| Food | Vitamin A per Serving | Notes |
|------|----------------------|-------|
| Efo Riro (Vegetable Soup) | 650mcg per bowl | One of the richest sources |
| Ugwu Leaves | 450mcg per 100g | Incredibly nutrient-dense |
| Edikang Ikong Soup | 450mcg per bowl | Premium but worth it |
| Efo Tete | 380mcg per 100g | Everyday affordable source |
| Efo Shoko | 350mcg per 100g | Good and widely available |
| Red Palm Oil | 300-500mcg per tbsp | The traditional Nigerian source — used in stews and soups |
| Banga Soup | 350mcg per bowl | Rich in palm fruit extract |
| Fried Plantain (Dodo) | 120mcg per serving | Beta-carotene from ripeness |
| Eggs | 80mcg per egg | Consistent source |

### Key Insight: Palm Oil is a Superfood for Vitamin A
Red palm oil, widely used in Nigerian cooking, is one of the richest natural sources of beta-carotene in the world. A single tablespoon provides 30-50% of the daily Vitamin A requirement. This is why traditional Nigerian diets that include palm oil-based stews and soups have a natural advantage in preventing Vitamin A deficiency.

## Vitamin C

### Why Vitamin C Matters
Vitamin C supports immune function, wound healing, and iron absorption. It is water-soluble and needs daily replenishment.

### Daily Vitamin C Requirements
- Men: 90mg/day
- Women: 75mg/day
- Pregnant women: 85mg/day

### Best Nigerian Vitamin C Sources
| Food | Vitamin C per Serving | Notes |
|------|----------------------|-------|
| Fresh Peppers (Ata Rodo/Tatase) | 40-80mg per serving | Used in virtually every Nigerian meal |
| Ugwu Leaves | 40mg per 100g | Also provides iron and calcium |
| Efo Riro | 35mg per bowl | From peppers and vegetables |
| Edikang Ikong | 40mg per bowl | Nutrient powerhouse |
| Efo Tete | 35mg per 100g | Affordable and rich |
| Boiled Plantain | 15mg per serving | Moderate but helpful |
| Fried Plantain (Dodo) | 18mg per serving | Some loss from frying |
| Tomatoes (in stew) | 15mg per 100g | Present in most Nigerian stews |
| Oranges/Citrus | 70mg per fruit | The classic Vitamin C source |

### Key Insight: Nigerian Cooking Preserves Vitamin C Through Peppers
While Vitamin C is heat-sensitive, the generous use of fresh peppers (scotch bonnet/Ata Rodo and bell peppers/Tatase) in Nigerian cooking provides substantial Vitamin C. Soups and stews that are not overcooked retain significant amounts.

## Sodium

### Why Sodium Monitoring Matters
Excessive sodium is the single biggest dietary risk factor for hypertension in Nigeria. The WHO recommends less than 2,000mg of sodium per day, but the average Nigerian consumes 3,000-5,000mg daily.

### Major Nigerian Sodium Sources (Hidden and Obvious)
| Source | Sodium Content | Impact |
|--------|---------------|--------|
| Maggi/Knorr Cubes | 800-1,000mg per cube | The #1 hidden sodium source — 2-3 cubes per pot of soup |
| Indomie Noodles | 1,200mg per serving | Extremely high — the seasoning packet alone has 900mg |
| Suya Spice (Yaji) | 850mg per serving | Very high from salt and seasonings |
| Salt (Table) | 2,300mg per teaspoon | Direct addition |
| Fried Rice | 720mg per plate | From seasoning and soy sauce |
| Jollof Rice | 680mg per plate | Seasoning cubes + salt |
| Canned/Processed Foods | Variable | Always high |

### Low-Sodium Nigerian Foods
- Plain boiled eggs, yam, plantain
- Fresh vegetables (Ugwu, Efo Tete, Garden Egg)
- Plain Oatmeal
- Water, unsweetened Zobo (if made without much salt)
- Ewedu prepared without seasoning cubes

### Sodium Reduction Strategies for Nigerian Cooking
1. Replace seasoning cubes with natural spices: locust beans (iru/dawadawa), crayfish, ginger, garlic, thyme
2. Use half the salt called for in recipes
3. Rinse canned foods before use
4. Choose fresh protein over processed/smoked varieties
5. Taste food before adding salt
